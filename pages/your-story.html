Known by Jesus

Here I Am

John Sherrod · written at the end of Known by Jesus

I built a career on a faulty foundation and protected titles.

It was meant to prove I was more than enough, better than generations before.

It was for me, my pride and ego, maybe a little for my children.

Then, I found the end of myself in a lonely yard, tired and confused, where the shape of surrender formed,

and a voice asked, “Where are you?”

I was hiding in the make believe world that addiction supported, hazy, formless and void.

When anyone got close I reached for the make believe room where I was protected with the vows from childhood.

I was afraid of being known, even to know myself, because I’m not sure a true identity existed.

I put down being coach, boss, leader, pretender and the other non-sense that formed the make-believe version of who I was.

I knew all about God being good and the enemy being evil, taught that in church as a child.

The first one I let know me was God...even though I know He was with me all along.

Then came the men who stood in the gap, ready to walk with me through the hard parts I ignored so long.

Where I tried hardest was in recovering communities that needed men with testimony,

and what was driving me was fear of failure as much as being known, loved and accepted.

The measure I used on myself was “You can do more, your status is built on what you achieve.”

What came off was pretender, the pride and all that striving for empty promises and false hope.

And grace came as an understanding nod, a reassuring glance and words that were true and lasting.

Where I used to rush was in conversations because my time was so valuable.

When I slowed down, I heard a voice that mattered and lived life with the good people in my community.

Under my feet was green grass where the dogs played, the nice sidewalk where we took long walks and gym floor where the sweat gathered.

The line that became ground was “Blessed are the peacemakers.”

Into an ordinary day I carried the tension between the emerging believer and the struggle to surrender the shame, to live in the blessing of each.

Around my table now are family, a community of believers, surprising me with their depth and capacity to love.

I pulled out a chair for my brother, my friends, and for the man I didn’t know.

Some stayed; some moved on to a seat somewhere I couldn’t follow.

I no longer pretend to be right or wrong, richer still, or better than. Yes is yes, no is no.

I am gentle, kind, patient and true.

And when He asks where I am, I say: “I am here Lord.”

Here I am.

Where I’m From

I Am From

John Sherrod

I am from the hills of east Tennessee, the streets of New York, the big skies of Texas, and the beaches of southern California. Equal parts each. I am a Jew and Gentile, from Scotland, Ireland, England, Germany, Sweden, Denmark, and the Cherokee Nation. I am an American, through and through, a citizen of the world who migrated to the United States. I’m a junkyard dog and prized shepherd. I am from Village Green, Concord, and Farragut; the same place in East Tennessee. I am from the streets of Knoxville, the farms of Lawnville, the rice paddies of Viet Nam, and battlefields of wars gone by, from spiritual, mental, and physical places my generations of family know too well.

I am from a place that wants to hear you and desperately seeks to connect – yet I’m still battling to leave a place that haunts me, disconnects me, and leaves me isolated and lonely. Trust is so fragile for us – in this place we so desire. I’m from a place that sees you, even if through corrective lenses or at times, you’re still a little blurry. I can make out your shape and sense your goodness as I discard the contempt for the story of me I’ve hidden for so long.

I am part of the wannabees, pretenders, and the people in-the-middle-of-it-all part of our country. I’m from the lower middle class trying not to be poor again, working to be more than before. I am Mountain Dew and Little Debbie. I am a dogwood and a bluebonnet, a rose and a rose by any other name, a weeping willow and forgotten tulip poplar. I am from football on Thanksgiving Day and gifts on Christmas morning, from strawberry pie and chicken and dumplings, from a family that sings Amazing Grace, the National Anthem, Just as I Am and I Surrender All, each with conviction and a sense of “this is who we are”.

I am hard work, long hours, and going all-in. When you fail, go all-in again. I am from Dana, Ryan and Madi, from Spot, Nicholas, Rex, Hagrid, Joker, Ren, and B. I’m from fear to freedom, from addiction to recovery, from isolation to community. I am from heaven to hell then back to heaven again. I am from the kingdom here on earth. I am from the place where every circumstance offers its blessing if you’re willing to see it in light of the good it wants to be. I’m from the promise of what this breath offers me.

Where I’m From

Jason Posey

I am the evening breeze whispering through summer warmed branches and leaves, as the cicadas sing their song of a brief life above the dirt.

I am the click and the clack of playing cards in the spokes of a bike wheel, fun at first but then pulled in irritation.

I am the smack of a bat sending a tennis ball towards the clouds, arcing down onto a neighbor’s windshield.

I am the scent of bubblegum and dollar store lip glaze smeared on my cheek, a kiss from a girl, teasing compliments from beyond my shyness.

My heart is the summer wind; unstoppable, unknowable, and free.

My soul is the setting summer sun, climbing down from the heights of the day, easing into the cooler night and its adventures offered in the dark.

My spirit is too wild for indoors, refusing to go home until absolutely left with no other choice, answering the call of my father.

I am an adult, looking back longingly and sadly.

I am a question; where did it all go?

I am a man capable of terrific force and action, but longing for peace.

I am a heart stripped of innocence, longing for purity and nobility of purpose.

I am a husband to a royal queen, born of a bloodline that tickles my conscience with its ancientness.

I am a father to children that deserved so much more than I gave.

I am a grandfather, amazed at how these little faces can cause my heart to so greatly swell.

I am just a man…saved by the most Powerful Being to ever exist.

Where I’m From

Where I’m From

Josiah Choiniere

I am from Glass Figurines and Lladros, from Diet Pepsi and Mountain Dew. I am from the beaches of Florida with white sand and crystal clear blue water, and from the beaches of California with brown sand and icy dark blue waters. I am from the Zugspitze and snow, whose mountain peak was above the clouds. I’m from the Patton Hotel and a love of surprises, from Jim and Kathy. I’m from it’s time to move and it’s time to laugh. From Keep that lip out and a little birdie will come poop on it and lying is an abomination. I’m from a multitude of churches across 2 continents and 6 states. I’m from Abilene TX and the military. I am from Family movie night and twister dinners, from my first love Kelly and my best friend Sergio. I am from Salzburg and Legoland and the best family trips of my childhood. I am from pipe tobacco and Werther’s candy, from seafood enchiladas, breakfast at the 50’s café with eggs, bacon, biscuits with strawberry jelly and 50’s music. From the grandfather that got shot on the beaches of Anguar that actually saved his life, from the grandmother that was disowned for loving my grandfather. I am from pictures on the wall or on shelves to share with anyone that visits, with an understanding that these items lead to memories and people who are always more important than things.
