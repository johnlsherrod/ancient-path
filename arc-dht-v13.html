The Man Who Crossed

Do Hard Things was one path into the work you have been doing, not the whole of it. Before Peter walked on water there was another storm: the disciples afraid, Jesus asleep, until they woke Him and He spoke to the wind and the waves. That storm taught them who He was, and it is what let Peter, in the next one, hear “Come” and step out of the boat. Twelve parts worked the same way: not one act of strength, but lesson after lesson, each one Christ used to prepare you for what came next.

The Man Who Crossed is where you name what that preparation built, and what the last hard thing — the crossing — cost. Twenty-five lines, five parts. Each line starts for you; you finish it in your own words. What comes out the other side is your crossing as a poem — from the shore you stood on to the table you sit at now. Where I’m From asks where you came from. This asks what you did.

Short is better. Write what you could point at — a place, a person, something someone said, what your body did. One picture per idea.
